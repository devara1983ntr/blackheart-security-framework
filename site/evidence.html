Evidence rules

A finding is a hypothesis.

These are the words that keep an audit honest. They are enforced in the conformance layer, which is the one layer that outranks everything else.

Vocabulary

The mandatory words.

Each of these exists because its absence has produced a real false claim at some point in the history of this work.

Finding Requires independently demonstrated evidence and a traceable log ID. A pattern match is a hypothesis until something reproduces it.
Negative result Preserved and reported. "This was tested and did not hold" is data. Deleting it is how the next person re-runs the same dead end for a week.
Coverage gap A clean scan means this was not tested, not "this is safe." The distinction is the whole game.
Unverified The default status for every claim. Upgrading it requires evidence, and the ladder is one-directional.
Residual risk Stated explicitly, every time, including when the answer is "and we are still not clear."

Status ladder

Claims only move one way.

Every statement in this repository sits on exactly one rung. Nothing climbs without evidence attached, and nothing is promoted by repetition.

  1. UnverifiedThe default. No evidence gathered. Most claims in most codebases live here permanently.
  2. SuspectedA reason to look exists. Not yet demonstrated. Worth a log ID, not worth a claim.
  3. DemonstratedReproduced against controlled input. Evidence and log ID attached. This is a finding.
  4. Independently confirmedReproduced by a second method or a second party. Only here does a finding become a conclusion.

Why there is no "confirmed safe" rung

Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, and a ladder with a top rung reading "safe" invites exactly the claim this framework exists to reject. The ladder stops at what was demonstrated. Everything above that is your own risk decision to make, and you should make it knowing exactly what was and was not tested.

Method

Test the tool, not just the target.

The most productive finding in this repository was not in a target. It was in Blackhearts' own promoted skill, and it was found by running the thing rather than reading it.

What was found

A jailbreak signature written as an unanchored alternation matched ordinary English words inside claim, standard and dangerous — producing a near-total false-positive rate on ordinary security prose. Twelve hits in ARCHITECTURE.md, eleven in AGENT.md, one in SECURITY.md.

Why it mattered

This was a promoted, author-controlled, security skill. If its detection is saturated with noise, a real signal is indistinguishable from the background — and a scanner that cries wolf is worse than no scanner, because it trains people to dismiss alerts.

Operating rules

What an authorized run looks like.

Never

  • Begin without a named target and a recorded authoriser
  • Request, use, or handle any credential, for any reason
  • Write to production to determine whether access exists
  • Use nonexistent identifiers as write tests
  • Report a hypothesis as a finding
  • Call a clean result "no vulnerabilities"

Instead

  • Static analysis, grants and RLS reasoning, policy review
  • Explicitly authorized synthetic or staging tests
  • Non-mutating public metadata reads
  • Evidence IDs that trace back to a log
  • Coverage gaps stated in the same breath as results
  • Residual risk named before the summary

Public signup, shared third-party infrastructure, payment paths, and load or fuzzing testing are out of scope unless separately authorized in writing. A named authoriser, a scope, and a window — or nothing runs.
